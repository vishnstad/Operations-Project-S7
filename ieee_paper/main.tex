\begin{abstract}
Land is fixed, yet crops differ sharply in yield, and land is usually allocated by habit
rather than by comparing district-level yield records. This paper formulates crop-area
allocation as two linear programs (LP): maximising total output for a fixed land budget, and
minimising the land needed for a production target. All coefficients are taken from the
Government of India district-wise, season-wise crop production record. Crops are unit-checked
before being pooled, yields are averaged across years, and both models are solved
computationally and compared with the historical allocation. For Karnataka's Kharif season
(1998--2019), the optimal plan raises output by 21.6\% on the same land, or meets historical
output on 23.9\% less land.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
linear programming, crop planning, land allocation, agricultural resource planning
\end{IEEEkeywords}

% =====================================================================
\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================
Yield per hectare varies widely across crops, districts and years, and land is limited: one
crop's gain is another's loss. Historical records exist at district--season--year
granularity but are rarely used to re-plan the next season's allocation. This work asks:
\emph{using actual historical yield records, how should land be re-allocated across crops to
get the most out of a fixed area?}

The objectives are to:
\begin{itemize}
  \item use the dataset's own Yield values as coefficients;
  \item formulate crop allocation as an LP in two forms, maximising output and minimising land~\cite{taha2017};
  \item solve both computationally;
  \item compare the optimal allocation with the actual historical allocation.
\end{itemize}

The scope is a single state and a single season, with yields averaged over several years.
Because the dataset has no cost or price data, the objective is output or land only, not
profit or cost.

\section{Dataset}
% =====================================================================
The source is the \emph{District-wise, Season-wise Crop Production Statistics} of the
Ministry of Agriculture \& Farmers Welfare~\cite{moafw}. It has 575{,}879 rows and 8 fields:
State, District, Crop Year (1997--2020), Season, Crop, Area (ha), Production and Yield, where
Yield $=$ Production$/$Area.

\textbf{Data does not give us:} cost or price data, so no profit or cost objective is possible.

\textbf{Data quirks, checked before modelling (Table~\ref{tab:clean}):}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Some crops are counted, not weighed: Coconut in nuts, and Cotton, Jute and Mesta in
        bales. They are excluded when crops are pooled into one output total.
  \item 212{,}373 records appear twice. The second copy has Production exactly $100\times$ the
        first (Fig.~\ref{fig:stacked}). The second copy is rescaled and the duplicate dropped.
  \item After this fix, yields match Production in tonnes (rice 2.0--2.8\,t/ha).
  \item 1997 and 2020 are partial years, so the analysis window is 1998--2019.
\end{itemize}

\section{Formulation}
% =====================================================================
For the crops $c$ grown in the chosen state and season, restricted to crops reported in the
same production unit, let:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $x_c$ be the hectares given to crop $c$;
  \item $y_c$ be its Yield averaged over the available years;
  \item $a_c$ and $m_c$ be its historical minimum and maximum area.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Optimization 1 (maximise output):} given a fixed land budget $L$, how many hectares
per crop maximise total production?
\begin{align}
\max\ Z=\textstyle\sum_c y_c x_c \quad
\text{s.t.}\ \textstyle\sum_c x_c\le L,\ \ a_c\le x_c\le m_c,\ \ x_c\ge0. \label{eq:o1}
\end{align}

\textbf{Optimization 2 (minimise land):} to guarantee a minimum production $D$, what is the
smallest land footprint?
\begin{align}
\min\ \textstyle\sum_c x_c \quad
\text{s.t.}\ \textstyle\sum_c y_c x_c\ge D,\ \ a_c\le x_c\le m_c,\ \ x_c\ge0. \label{eq:o2}
\end{align}

Both models use the same data and the same $y_c$; only the objective and the binding
constraint are swapped, which is the primal/dual pairing for a resource-allocation LP.
$L$ and $D$ default to the historical land and output.

excluded because they are reported in bales. Table~\ref{tab:res} summarises both models.

\textbf{Optimization 1.} The plan in Fig.~\ref{fig:opt1}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item raises Potato, Onion, Maize, Rice, Ragi, Jowar and Soyabean to their historical maximum;
  \item holds the lower-yield pulses, oilseeds and millets at their minimum.
\end{itemize}
The value of one extra hectare (the land dual) is 0.81\,t.

\textbf{Optimization 2.} Historical output can be produced on 1.46 million ha less land.
The dual of the target is 0.3806\,ha/t. This equals the reciprocal of the land dual of
Opt.~1 at that land level, which confirms the primal/dual pairing.

\section{Sensitivity and Multi-District Comparison}
% =====================================================================
\textbf{Land budget.} Varying $L$ shows diminishing returns: optimal output rises ever more
slowly, and the value of extra land falls in steps as crops reach their upper bounds
(Fig.~\ref{fig:param}).

\textbf{Bounds.} The gain depends on how far each crop may move. If each crop may change by
only $\pm10\%$ from its historical area, the gain is 6.2\% more output or 5.0\% less land,
which is consistent with the 3.75--15\% reported in~\cite{supriya2022}.

\textbf{Districts.} Solving both models for each of 27 districts gives a median gain of
25.8\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:multi}). The gain is highest in the diverse dry districts (Koppal
+65\%, Gadag +55\%) and lowest in the coastal rice districts (Udupi +0.2\%).

\section{Assumptions and Limitations}
% =====================================================================
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Production units must match.} Crops counted in nuts or bales are excluded
        from the pooled total.
  \item \textbf{Historical averages.} Yields are averaged over years; weather variation is not
        modelled.
  \item \textbf{Output, not profit.} No cost or price field exists in the dataset.
  \item \textbf{Not modelled:} soil quality, irrigation, labour, crop rotation, and price or
        demand shifts.
\end{enumerate}

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% =====================================================================
Using only area, production and yield from the government record, the two LP models show
that Karnataka's Kharif land could have produced 21.6\% more output, or the same output on
23.9\% less land. Unit checks and duplicate-record checks applied before modelling were
essential to obtaining valid coefficients.
